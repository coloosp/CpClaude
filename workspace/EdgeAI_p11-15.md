## 6.1.1. DNN inference accelerator in binary offloading

Deploying DNNs with high-performance efficiency on edge devices is challenging due to their high computational and space complication. In this context, there are three major research dimensions for achieving on-device computation and storage, namely pruning (network weights, neurons, channels, filters, etc.), quantization, knowledge distillation, and their hybrid combinations. These approaches reduce the size and complexity of the DL model, therefore, turns them efficient for edge devices.

• Pruning: Numerous high-performing DNN models utilize extensive neurons and synaptic connections, resulting in substantial computational and storage complexity. In contrast, an abundance of these are redundant, wherein eliminating them significantly reduces computation and space complexity without affecting the accuracy (Liang et al., 2021a; Cheng et al., 2024). The pruning technique determines the redundant connections or weights, neurons, channels, or filters in a DNN model and eliminates them to make the DNN model more compact (He et al., 2017; Luo et al., 2017). Le et al. presented a brainyEdge (Le et al., 2023) mechanism that utilizes weight pruning at each layer to the deployment phase of the feedforward neural network in an automated surface inspection system on an assembly line. Similarly, Zhu et al. (2020) revised the DenseNet architecture for automatic defect detection at the resource-limited edge by removing redundant connections in the DL model through pruning. Ashiquzzaman et al. (2020) applied filter pruning based on the L1 normalization in hand gesture recognition using CNN. It compresses the CNN model by 1.05% and 50% less computation while maintaining the same accuracy of 98%, resulting in lightweight mobile edge-based server applications. These mechanisms retrains the unpruned parameters for some iterations to reduce loss incurred from pruning. The layerwise filter pruning mandates computationally extensive sensitivity analysis and leads to the accumulation of pruning errors, directing performance reduction of pruned DNNs. Naveen et al. (2021) discussed a low-latency DNN inference model for a distributed edge environment. The work formulated latency depreciation as a pruning problem and utilized weight pruning followed by finetuning to acquire an optimized DL model to reduce inference latency. These pruning procedures are also extended for ensemble and semi-supervised learning techniques such as EnSeMUp in quality prediction tasks (Nain et al., 2024a). Yu et al. addressed it through EasiEdge (Yu et al., 2020), a global pruning method that quantifies the impact of filter removal by information gain (IG). Pruning 80% of VGG-16 filters reduces inference latency on Jetson TX2 CPU from 76.85 to 8.01 ms with only a 0.22% accuracy drop. In addition, CroApp (Jia et al., 2022) optimizes the CNN model’s resources through channel pruning and achieves an overall resource optimization by training a pool of shareable models established on these pruned models.

• Early exit: A branchy network architecture is extended to have supplementary side branch classifiers (Teerapittayanon et al., 2016). Li et al. (2018a) introduced DeepIns with the concept of early exit (branches) in the DNN to leverage the early inference at the edge devices. The edge nodes extract lower-level features and infer the output therein. The intermediate values are forwarded for the entire CNN inference at remote cloud servers only when the output at the edge cannot meet the requirements. This reduces the communication cost and the inference dependency on the cloud. The other authors extended this concept by incorporating multi-agents in the branchy DNNs to ensure production process optimization (Lin et al., 2020). The optimal selection of the early exit point of the DNN model in edge-cloud architecture also plays a significant role. Therefore, Wang et al. (2023c) proposed a segmentation mechanism for Branchy DNNs (DNNs with early exit feature) to determine the type and position of the fault in printed circuit boards. Jiao et al. (2024) discussed a CNNbased mechanism to accurately identify the location of litchi fruit among the collected images. It utilizes the channel pruning, layer pruning, and knowledge distillation to reduce model size.

• Quantization: Quantization is another compression mechanism that represents the weights, gradients, and activations of the DNN model through fewer fixed-point numbers instead of 32- bit floating-point representation (Micikevicius et al., 2017; Han et al., 2015). It includes arithmetic operations that consume less power and resources, significantly reducing hardware complexity. TIP4.0 is a predictive maintenance platform that includes quantization to reduce the DNN model size and performs inference four times faster than the uncompressed model on the CPU (Resende et al., 2021). Gil et al. (2021) utilized an effective quantization-aware DNN pruning method that runs on small and cost-effective industrial devices. The proposed UVNQ approach delivered a pruning procedure to be applied for bit precision before performing the successive quantization. To provide a hardware prototype, the data quantization module can be implanted onto edge AI chips that are adaptive and lowpower consumption necessitating mechanism (Li et al., 2022c). The researchers validated the effectiveness of their algorithm and hardware modules for precise navigation and accurate classification for autonomous vehicles. Balaskas et al. (2024) described an energy-efficient inference solution for implanted DNN accelerators to cater to the demand for executing sophisticated DNN-based inferences on resource-scarce embedded devices. To this end, an automated framework is devised that jointly utilizes the pruning and quantization schemes to attain hardware-aware DNN compression in an energy-efficient manner. Moreover, RL is employed for identifying the pruning-quantization composition to obtain a trade-off between energy consumption and inference prediction accuracy (Yang et al., 2023). Rakib et al. (2024) employed an Int-8 quantization lightweight Q-CNN based solution to detect plant disease.

• Knowledge Distillation (KD): It is a procedure in which the knowledge from a large or teacher model is transferred to smaller ones (Gou et al., 2021). In this method, a lightweight and efficient smaller student model is trained specifically for EDs using a large teacher so that the small model learns the same information. Fang et al. (2020) demonstrated an on-demand DNN-based inference methods for on-premise EDs called EdgeKE. It utilizes the benefits of DNN compression based on KD (to improve accuracy and speed), further acceleration through early exit (to provide flexible choices of latency and accuracy). Others performed DLbased fault diagnosis on embedded systems in real-time using vibration signals by training a lightweight student network using the MobileNet V3 model (He et al., 2023). It reduced computation cost by 170 times with an overall accuracy rate of 94.37%, and run time was diminished by 34%. Putrada et al. (2023) proposed an edge smart lighting (EdgeSL) architecture for real-time smart lighting control in which KD-based compression methods are introduced for the k-nearest neighbor (KNN) model called DistilKNN. Indeed, they claimed it had achieved better performance than pruning and quantization. Similarly, the larger teacher model is trained in the cloud and used for secondary verification of alarm system warnings only for higher-level warning states in the smart factory. The student model at the edge performs the primary verification of most inputs (Oh et al., 2022).

Discussion: The above discussion comprises the various solutions to obtained a compressed version of DNN, whose resource requirements can be adapted by resource poor devices. HowHowever, DNN compression directly affects the model performance (such as accuracy loss), which may hamper the performance of DL-based IoT applications where model performance is essential (such as quality inspection, medical imaging, etc.). The schemes such as knowledge distillation or iterative pruning imposes the additional training overhead. The compression schemes also face deployment challenges due to the requirement of specific hardware and libraries for execution. The next section explores the partial computation offloading based solution to address the issues of binary-offloading-based DNN inference execution.

## 6.2. Partial offloading

The partial offloading-based solution decomposes a task into smaller subtasks and distributes them to the underlying computing infrastructure for execution. DNN splitting is a research domain enabling end-edge synergic implementation of DNN inference tasks. The synergy among the end and edge devices is named collaborative computing, which caters to near-real-time requirements (Gauttam et al., 2023). The splitting point needs to be determined in these approaches, which are based on partial computational offloading. This is the point at which the DNN layers are partitioned among collaborative devices (such as ECN and ES) and relieve the computational burdens at these edge devices. Besides, the ECN sends the intermediate outcomes from the DNN layers (layers executing at the ECN) to the ES. This is achieved considering the transmission of smaller sizes data (intermediate outcomes from ECN) instead of the actual raw data, decreasing data transmission delays. DNN splitting improves overall task execution time by optimizing computation and communication resources. This section explores the various SL-based DNN inference task execution solutions.

Kang et al. (2017) presented a DNN model partitioning mechanism between a mobile edge device and a cloud server in order to diminish latency or energy consumption. This partitioning method chooses the best splitting point depending on DNN profiling parameters (which include per-layer execution time for both device and cloud, as well as per-layer output size) and network bandwidth. After partitioning point selection, the model layers before this point are executed on mobile device and remaining on the cloud. The DNN partitioning can be employed with a right-sizing mechanism to maintain the trade-off between inference latency and prediction performance (Li et al., 2019). Zhou et al. (2019) developed a DL model partitioning mechanism utilizing dynamic programming within a multi-level IoT architecture, aiming to diminish response time. This mechanism divides the DNN layers among devices at different levels, taking into account network bandwidth and computing resources. It aligns with the framework presented in Kang et al. (2017) for device-edge, device-cloud, and edge-cloud model splitting scenarios. Boomerang is developed as an edge intelligence framework that provides on-demand cooperative DNN inference under the resource-constrained IIoT environment, executing model inference with improved latency and accuracy (Zeng et al., 2019). The early exit technique employed in Boomerang reduce the DNN inference latency while ensuring inference accuracy. Dividing the DNN computation between the IoT devices and the ES leverages hybrid computing resources and lower the model inference time. Shan et al. (2020) designed a framework named Cogent to accelerate the DNN inference task. The framework employs an RL-based solution to predict the DNN splitting and pruning to minimize the inference latency based on hardware conditions and system configuration. Saguil and Azim (2020) developed a framework that enables the creation of several DNNs and chains them together. It utilizes the concept of DNN right sizing and splitting to reduce the inference latency. The splitting points in each model are determined by discovering the areas with the largest differences in consecutive layer execution time.

The coEdge (Hu et al., 2020) framework exploits the capabilities of edge and cloud in a collaborative manner to reduce the DNN inference latency. The authors formulate the DNN splitting problem as a latency minimization problem and devise an approximate solution to achieve the optimal DNN split point in polynomial time. Some work, such as Matsubara et al. (2020), extended the DNN splitting solutions to an efficient DNN inference acceleration mechanism that integrates the characteristics of DNN splitting and Knowledge Distillation. The scheme first splits the DNN into front and back-end layers to reduce the computational burden on the end device. Further, it utilizes KD to compress the front-end DNN to improve the DNN inference performance.

DeepWear, a DL framework for wearable devices, is proposed by Xu et al. (2019) to improve DNN inference task execution performances by partitioning the computation between the wearable and paired handheld devices. It considers DNN profiling at wearable (for example, watches, etc.), handheld device, and bandwidth for partitioning while minimizing task execution latency and energy consumption. Chen et al. (2021a) introduced a computational offloading scheme called DNNoff for edge-enabled IoT systems employing DNN models. It employs a Random Forest (RF) model to obtain the DNN profiling and partitioned the layers between device-edge and device-cloud based on the RF-based profiling outcomes and available bandwidth to reduce the total response time. Li et al. (2018b) examined a DL computational offloading method for an edge-enabled IoT scenario wherein a method is deployed to split the DNN model in edge-cloud collaboration. They attained the objective of maximizing the tasks deployed at the edge while assuring the QoS necessities. Similar work for DNN-IoT applications discussed in Chen et al. (2021a) delivered a DNN splitting scheme for offloading computational burden while minimizing the task response time. Assuming a DNN task combined many sub-tasks, they were executed sequentially to preserve the layerwise sequence of DNN model configurations. They embraced the Shortest Job First (SJF) based greedy method for scheduling the computing sub-task in edge-cloud collaboration, reducing response time. Identically, Shi et al. (2021) employed greedy strategy-based DNN partitioning in a deviceedge collaboration for minimizing task execution latency in multi-task settings. Yun et al. (2022) designed a memory-aware DNN inference acceleration solution for memory and delay-constrained wireless IoT networks. The solution utilizes KD to construct a smaller student DNN model from a large teacher DNN to cope with the constrained memory of IoT devices. It consists of a dynamic DNN selection-based collaborative DNN inference that optimizes the DNN (student/teacher), the partitioning point for the preferred DNN, and the intermediate data transmission rate.

Xue et al. (2021) also designed an efficient offloading scheme for DNN inference acceleration in a device-edge-cloud cooperative setting to realize the real-time DNN query. It utilizes a PSO and GA empowered hybrid solution and a layer merge uploading algorithm to obtain DNN migration and partition plan and maintain efficient query performance. enabling Distributed DNN (eDDNN) (Huang et al., 2021), are designed for distributed DNN inference schemes in an environment of heterogeneous end devices. It leverages a cross-platform WebRTC protocol that splits tasks horizontally, allowing the DNN model to be executed nearly independently on end devices by sharing a DNN dependency table. The solution also comprises an RL-based dynamic task allocation algorithm to efficiently utilize the computing power of the collaborative fielddevices that reduce the overall latency and lower the computational burden on ESs. Chen et al. (2021b) designed an energy-efficient offloading technique using a self-adaptive PSO algorithm based on the GA operators to divide the DNN layers while reducing system energy consumption and ensuring deadline constraints.

The task partitioning and offloading schemes can be employed collectively for a DNN-task-enabled MEC network comprising a single server and numerous mobile devices (MDs) to reduce inference delay and energy consumption (Gao et al., 2021). The optimal DNN offloading must be attained with load balancing, and therefore, Xu et al. (2022) described a distributed auction algorithm that attained it them in polynomial time to reduce the inference latency and efficient utilization of edge resources. Yang and Wei (2022) designed a coalition formation method to achieve privacy and energy-aware split learning in edge camera networks to infer DL tasks on the computationally poor edge-IoT system. The solution performs user association decisions, resource allocation for edge cameras, and device configuration with DL model parameter selection. It utilizes a coalition formation gamebased scheme to solve the association problem between edge nodes and cameras. Further, a split DL solution is adopted for flexible allocation of computing and communication resources. Thus, the coalition formation and split DL jointly maximize the multi-view detection, privacy preference, and energy savings of cameras. Liu et al. (2023b) jointly explored a model partitioning solution with a mobility-aware computation offloading solution among the cooperative devices by considering the characteristics of Vehicular EC (VEC). These characteristics are higher mobility, dynamic network topology, and irregular wireless connections to reduce DNN inference latency and ensure reliability. Nature-inspired optimization schemes such as Improved Particle

Swarm Genetic mechanism (Li et al., 2023a) determined an optimized computational offloading strategy through DNN partitioning to reduce the overall task execution latency in VEC. Feltin et al. (2023) formulated the DNN partitioning problem as a MINLP. It employs a branch and bound solver to partition the given DNN model to maximize the throughput of DNN inference tasks and cater to the latency and privacysensitive requirements of DL-based IoT applications. Dynamic Adaptive DNN Surgery mechanism enabled the split DNN to be executed in edge and cloud synergy while reducing the data transmission (Liang et al., 2023) and optimally splitting the model under varying network scenarios. In addition, they analyzed the DNN splitting in a costconstrained scenario such that the cloud consists of limited resources for the DNN inference task. It employed DNN Surgery Light and Heavy to attain optimal DNN partition under lightly and heavily loaded networking scenarios. DNN splitting is merged with the resource allocation optimization approaches to reduce the execution of model inference tasks in the edge or edge-cloud IoT scenarios (Fan et al., 2023). The DL model splitting is modeled as MINLP and divided into two smaller subproblems due to the higher computational cost. The first subproblem is solved using graph-based device clustering for reducing the data transmission delay between device and base station. Next, the second subproblem utilizes DNN splitting and resource allocation mechanisms jointly to minimize computing and transmission delay between the base stations and the cloud. Su et al. (2022) investigate a joint optimization problem in a hierarchical energy-constrained edge–cloud environment for the minimization of the average end-to-end delay of various DL tasks while ensuring that the energy consumption of the ES and cloud remains within the allocated energy budgets. The authors achieved it through an RL-based solution to dynamically partition the DNN by monitoring the environment. Dong et al. (2022) presented an investigation, concluding that the performance of a DNN inference task is influenced collectively by exit point determination, DNN partitioning, and resource allocation in edge AI scenario. It employs a bidirectional dynamic programming empowered exit point selection algorithm. it also employs a DRL-based scheme that learned from offloading decisions and determined optimal resource allocation and DNN splitting point. Wang et al. (2023a) designed a dynamic optimization approach for DNN splitting under the scenarios of fluctuating wireless channel(s) and random DNN task arrival. They addressed the challenges of varying wireless channel conditions and DNN tasks in multiuser MEC systems and minimized the average latency of inference tasks. The dynamic DNN task workload at end devices and MEC server is modeled using a local CPU and MEC server workload queue. It dynamically determines the partitioning of the local DNN task at the wireless device using a parametric online Q-learning algorithm by considering the instantaneous status of channel conditions, end device status, and status of MEC server, respectively, to reduce the average latency of the DNN inference tasks.

Jung and Lee (2023) developed a framework for low-latency DNN inference task computation over distributed computing platforms to reduce inference latency. It utilizes routing in the layered graph model to enable the selection of nodes for computation and paths for data transfer. Others proposed a wireless channel adaptive DNN partition framework (Lee et al., 2023) in energy and memory constraints edge environment, minimizing the inference latency. The framework employs a two-stage DNN splitting, where DNN splitting points for the deployment are decided offline by considering the mean of transmission latency. Next, it adaptively adjusts the deployed DNN model on the edge-cloud synergy in an online fashion based on the instantaneous channel conditions. Dong et al. (2023) discussed a decentralized DRL scheme to accelerate the DNN inference by collaborating resourceconstrained IoT nodes and mobile edge computing. They optimized distributed task offloading for intelligent edge or end devices to tackle the computation and communication challenges of DNN inference execution using a multi-agent RL-based solution that selects an early exit point in the DL model for balancing accuracy and latency. Such solutions are diversified in the applications of resource-constrained Internet of Vision Things (Xu et al., 2023b) for edge-device collaborative inference to accomplish DNN-based inference efficiently. They collectively adopted early exit architectures with resource allocation schemes and modeled this joint problem to speed up the DNN inference while ensuring the desired inference accuracy. They employed a MDP and a deep deterministic policy gradient approach to cater to the above-mentioned issues in an edge-device collaborative set-up.

A novel approach, known as NNFacet (Chen et al., 2023a), is a DNN splitting framework to conduct a DNN-based classification task on ?? simultaneous battery-resource-constrained sensors with reduced latency and energy consumption. NNFacet divides a large multi-class DL model into several smaller models wherein each small model is deployed on a resource-constrained smart sensor that learns a subset of the classes. The final inference is generated by fusing the inference of sensor nodes produced by their submodel. Duan and Wu (2023) presented a solution which collectively optimize DNN partition and pipelines the scheduling for various DNN inference jobs for minimizing the latency. The work designed a near-optimal splitting and scheduling strategies based on binary search, and idea of topology sort for chainstructure extended them for general-structure DNNs. Wu et al. (2023) discussed a splitting mechanism for DNN consisting of Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) topology to minimize the inference latency. The solution employs a topological sort-based mechanism to convert DAG-topology DNNs into chain-topology DNNs and applies a greedy approach to partition and offload chain DNNs. Considering a heterogeneous dynamic IoT environment, Samikwa et al. (2023) discussed a distributed micro-split DL to attain the optimized trade-off between latency and energy consumption. The solution comprises a heuristic-based mechanism that iteratively performs vertical and horizontal DNN partitioning to carry out DNN execution in a distributed manner in the network of IoT nodes with diversified computing abilities and varying network situations without compromising the DNN model performance. Lim et al. (2024) addressed the combined optimized dynamic DNN model splitting, processing, and network resources to enhance the quality of experience (QoE) for DL-based IoT applications in the edge computing paradigm. The authors designed an online and dynamic DNN splitting mechanism to decrease the energy consumption and average endto-end latency of the DNN inference tasks. Consequently, Li et al. (2024a) studied it further for fine-grained model partitioning among the collaboration of ES-IoT assets to address the challenges of the DNN inference execution task at a heterogeneous edge-IoT environment. The DNN splitting problem is formulated as the MDP, for maximizing the long-term ignored cumulative reward of distributed DNN inference tasks. The authors utilized a multi-task learning-based asynchronous advantage actor-critic approach to finding the optimal DNN splitting policy to reduce DNN inference execution delay.

Li and Bi (2024) examined an RL-based efficient DNN splitting solution to attain device-edge collaborative DNN inference for multiuser wireless sensing to enable the accurate detection of various human activities through the analysis of various radio signal variations of sensor devices in near-real time. PArtNNer (Ghosh et al., 2024), is a system that enables distributed DNN inference in a single ED-cloud scenario to reduce DNN inference latency. The scheme determines the decision to drive the inference task exclusively on edge, entirely in cloud, or in edge-cloud collaboration by analyzing the influence of temporally altering underlying operating conditions, computing, and communication architecture, respectively. Ko et al. (2024) designed a Two-Phase Split Computing (TPSC) framework to reduce the inference latency of DNN inference tasks. The framework utilizes inter-layer and intra-layer splitting to attain the same, where inter-layer splitting performs the layerwise splitting and intra-layer splitting divides the computing task of a layer among the various nodes at the same level. Qi et al. (2024) discussed a Multi-Compression Scale DNN Inference Acceleration (MCIA) solution in end-edge-cloud collaboration in the multi-user multi-task environment to cope with the scenarios of distinct requirements of DNN inference services (for instance, highly accurate or latency-sensitive tasks). It deploys multiple versions of the DNN models with different compression scales at field devices and ED, and utilized a DRL algorithm for adaptive and collaborative inferences with optimal resource allocation.

Discussion: The above discussion involves the investigation and analysis of various partial-offloading-based DNN inference execution solutions (such as SL and its integration with other DNN inference accelerator (based on application requirements) schemes) to minimize the execution cost of the DNN inference task. The SL-based schemes attains the optimized utilization of the available computing and storage resource. These solutions transmit the intermediate data among collaborative devices, which addresses the data-privacy. The core SL-based solution (i.e. without any additional DNN accelerator) did not alter the DNN architecture that mitigates the model performance degradation. It demonstrates that these solutions are suitable for scenarios where maintaining model accuracy is critical, for example, quality inspection in smart manufacturing. However, model splitting introduce an additional overhead, which may degrades the performance of realtime applications, initiating the needs for lightweight model splitting schemes. ## 7. Model training optimization layer

This section discusses the challenges and respective solutions in the context of the third layer (model training optimization) of DEMMA. DNN training task2 comprises a DNN model and training data set. It aims to optimize the randomly initialized weights of a given DNN by minimizing a loss function using optimization algorithms (such as gradient descent) and backpropagation mechanism. Edge training (also referred to as edge learning) employs the solution that enables the training of DNN models on edge infrastructure. It reduces the dependency on the centralized cloud server for DNN training tasks. However, the limited resources (computing and storage) at the edge impose significant challenges for the successful execution of resourcehungry DNN training at the edge. This section presents an investigation and discussion of the contemporary literature, comprising the solutions utilized to address the challenges of DNN training task at the network edge. Fig. 8 depicts the categorization of DNN training task execution solutions in Edge-AI paradigm. The mechanisms broadly employs the solo (on-device/edge) and distributed/collaborative learning approaches. The DNN training is distributed among numerous EDs in split or distributed learning, whereas the on-device/edge learning executes the DNN training on individual ED. Further, these schemes integrates the DNN training accelerators such as model compression, KD, quantization, etc., for performance enhancement.

The choice of a DNN training execution scheme is affected by application requirements (e.g., convergence time, accuracy, and reliability) and available computational, storage, and network resources. For instance, in an autonomous vehicle driving scenario, a vehicleonly training mechanism (solo training) executes the model updates locally using onboard resources. It preserves data privacy and eliminates network dependency, but requires significant computing power and storage to achieve the desired model accuracy within an acceptable time frame. In contrast, an edge-only training scheme can support the training of complex models, but it increases communication overhead due to raw data transfer from vehicles to edge servers and may raise privacy concerns.
