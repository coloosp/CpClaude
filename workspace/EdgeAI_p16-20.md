## 7.1. Solo (on device/edge) DNN learning in Edge-AI

In the context of on-device learning, ME-FEEL (Tang et al., 2022) is a novel multi-exit federated edge learning that accomplishes the DL training and model weight sharing efficiently in a resource-constrained environment. The framework divides the DL model into different submodels of various depths and output prediction from the exits in their respective sub-model. It enables the selection of early exits and avoids training the complete model at poor computational devices, which, in turn, reduces model training latency. The performance characterization of DNN model training on various EDs (three contemporary classes of NVIDIA Jetson) by varying the device (such as storage media) and training parameters (such as batch size) is investigated in SK et al. (2022). Wang et al. (2023b) developed a training approach for Binary Neural Network (BNN) training that is low-cost and suitable for resource-constrained EDs. It is adaptive for attaining sizable memory footprint contractions at the cost of little to no accuracy drops compared to standard benchmarks. Kook et al. (2024) presented an efficient offloading architecture called joint data deepening-andprefetching to reduce the transmission cost of high-dimensional and voluminous data from resource-constrained IoT devices to EDs for DL training. It uploads the features of data based on importance rather than raw data, which reduces the energy consumption of data transmission. Further, the pre-fetching steps determine the potential features required for future training in advance to enhance precision and mitigate energy consumption overhead.

Yu et al. (2024) compiled the pruning approach with quantization to achieve quick training and/or retraining of new tools, machines, or processes with scarce data in the industrial anomaly prediction of equipment. The model size is diminished by about 60%, making it realizable for EDs like Raspberry Pi through pruning and quantization. Sung and Han (2024) discussed a model maintenance approach in the 5G IoT network for IoT-AI applications. The approach employs the concepts of model pruning and approximate learning to maintain the trade-off between model performance and resource consumption for DNN training. A DRL scheme obtains a lightweight DNN from the fullscale DNN model to attain learning from the online data based on the above trade-off. XBNet-Based Framework (Hamad, 2025), utilizing the collective features of ML and genetic algorithms, is proposed to enhance gene-disease classification. It combines the advanced feature extraction and selection technique, which improves the classification performance.

## 7.2. Distributed DNN learning in Edge-AI

This section discusses the distributed learning approaches, including Federated Learning (FL), Split Learning (SL), and concurrence of FL and SL to overcome the limitations of solo DNN learning schemes. The discussion also includes the acceleration methods utilized to speed up the distributed DNN training.

## 7.2.1. Federated learning

Data privacy is a vital concern in various scenarios, including healthcare, autonomous vehicles, smart manufacturing, and more. Hence, the data cannot be shared in the remote cloud for model training because it contains confidential or sensitive information or due to compliance and legal concerns. The efficient handling of distributed sub-datasets in the above-mentioned scenarios calls for decentralized learning solutions. In this context, a FL solution trains a local model at each edge site and aggregates their parameters at the global model at the ES or cloud without sharing the sensitive data (Wang et al., 2022a). Hence, each ED maintains the privacy of the data at each local site and prepares a global DNN model, which is updated at each ED. A FL framework is designed for the wirelessly connected swarm of UAVs, comprising a leader UAV and a set of followers UAVs (Zeng et al., 2020). Each follower UAV gathers data to train its own local DL model and forwards the revised model parameters to the leader UAV to generate the global model. Brik et al. (2022) utilized the FL to design a prediction model for resource localization of mobile resources in industrial systems. Some research, such as Li et al. (2022b), focused on optimizing the weight coefficients of clients involved in parameter aggregation. It involved utilizing an improved PSO and also embedded a method for selecting appropriate models (or the FL client) to update the global FL model. They propose a stacking model for diagnosing inter-turn short circuit faults in permanent magnet synchronous motors. Farahani and Monsefi (2023) discussed privacy-enhancing FL in the inter- and intra-organizational collaboration of IIoT data to present a holistic, decentralized architecture and validated its efficacy in an edgeenabled collaborative predictive diagnostics and maintenance case study. In addition, Putra et al. (2023) suggested a hierarchical federated transfer learning for simultaneously learning and detecting faults in the 3D-printing process that has 24% faster training time and 45% accuracy improvement compared to traditional FL architecture.

Accelerating the FL: FL is a novel training technique that protects data privacy by building shared learning of the DL/ML model from the training data that is locally available on distributed edge nodes. However, the model update in FL can be bandwidth-consuming as it requires parameter exchanges among edge nodes in iterative fashion. The communication cost among collaborative devices (ECN/ES/Cloud) is high when updating models through FL. Tonellotto et al. (2021) proposed the models to undergo a first quantization after being updated by the edge servers, followed by a second quantization before transferring global model from cloud node to the edge servers. Zheng et al. (2021) introduced a distributed hierarchical tensor deep computation model to reduce FL’s bandwidth consumption and storage requirement by reducing the DL model’s parameters from high to low-dimensional tensors. In a similar work, Wang et al. (2020d) integrated KD into FL to overcome privacy issues and transmission costs in object recognition tasks in IoT scenarios. Zhang et al. (2020) designed a big-little branch architecture inspired by BranchyNet to enable efficient FL for AIoT applications. The scheme deploys the little branches of the DNN model on field or IoT devices due to the availability of limited resources and big branches on cloud servers to improve prediction accuracy. Tanghatari et al. (2022) discussed an approach to enable distributed DNN training on resource-deprived IoT devices. A heuristic approach converts the large DNN model (teacher) into the smaller model (student) based on the available resource characteristics of the IoT node. The knowledge of the teacher is conveyed to the student model using KD. The global model is obtained through the aggregation of student model parameters at ES. The scheme reduces the data transmission cost between ES and IoT devices due to the transmission of fewer parameters of the student model and attains 3.5% accuracy loss compared to cloud-based training.

Mamba Kabala et al. (2023) employ the FL approach to train the attention-based CNN for disease detection to improve productivity, reduce costs, and promote environmentally friendly crop treatment techniques. De Rango et al. (2023) designed an energy-efficient hierarchical FL approach for the EC paradigm. The hierarchical architecturebased FL scheme exploits the benefits of available computing power at various layers of edge infrastructure for data aggregation, which, in turn, reduces the data transmission cost. An edge FL solution for the network of mobile nodes under the optimization of delay, energy, and cost for maximizing the probability of prosperous receiving of the local models at ES, respectively, is presented by Pervej et al. (2023). Lian et al. (2023) merged the features of FL and model sparsification to create a distributed, privacy-preserving, and communication-efficient solution for traffic sign recognition. Djenouri et al. (2023) designed an FL framework to preserve data privacy and reduce the training cost of a GCNN to improve the performance of traffic flow prediction in a smart city scenario. Besides this, Aggarwal et al. (2024) designed a resourceefficient, lightweight, and privacy-friendly FL framework by integrating the characteristics of transfer learning and feature extraction for Internet of Agriculture Things (IoAT) scenarios. FL has applications in predicting soil-based crop recommendations (Mwawado et al., 2024). In this work, the global model is initialized on a server and distributed to the Raspberry pi nodes to train the model on the respective local dataset. Each node shares the locally trained model with the server to obtain an efficient global model. Bera et al. (2024) discussed an IoTdew-edge-cloud collaborative latency and energy-aware FL framework for sustainable irrigation. The ESs accomplish the training of local models on respective datasets and transmit the revised parameters to a cloud server for aggregation. To reduce the per-round latency in FL (leading to a reduction of overall training time), Zhang et al. (2024a) utilized the gradient quantization approach. A DDRL scheme allocates the quantization level to maintain the trade-off between quantization error, model accuracy, and training time, respectively. Adjei-Mensah et al. (2024) introduced a novel attention-based feature extraction mechanism that enhances the image classification performance and integrates it with FL to ensure privacy-preserving model training using medical data.

## 7.2.2. Split learning

SL is a distributed DL framework that splits the DNN layers between the collaborative resource-constrained EDs to reduce the computational burden of EDs and preserve data privacy (Gupta and Raskar, 2018). Zhang and Xu (2021) discussed an efficient DNN partition framework for collaborative DNN model training to cater to privacy requirements. It comprises Learn to Split (L2S), an online approach that utilizes a bandit learning algorithm to optimally split the DNN layers dynamically, preserving data privacy and enhancing training performance. A reliable ML-based computational task offloading framework utilized the features of SL to ensure the data privacy and minimum data exchange between participating EDs (Bharti and McGibney, 2022). Akintoye et al. (2022) described a scalable and distributed DL mechanism by jointly employing features of data and model parallelism to speed up the training time. The authors devised a Genetic Algorithm Heuristic Resources Allocation scheme that splits the DNN model into multiple groups of layers and allocates them to available computing devices to reduce the computing cost and improve resource utilization. Samikwa et al. (2022) presented a resource-aware SL approach for distributed DNN training in an edge-IoT environment. The solution involves the underlying network and computational resources in the decision-making process of split-DNN layers among devices to minimize training time. Wang et al. (2023d) designed a privacypreserving SL framework, Masked Split Learning (MaskSL), to facilitate sensitive image pre-training with large-scale models in device-server synergy. It utilizes the concept of masked pre-training to minimize the computation and communication overheads. The mechanism masks patches based on Fisher information to improve training performance under limited bandwidth and privacy constraints. Further, to improve the scalability and memory efficiency of large-scale DNN training on large-scale computing clusters, Li et al. (2023b) designed a hybrid parallel training framework. The framework splits the DNN model using an integrated pipelined model and data parallelism to ensure high throughput, low memory overhead, and scalability in DNN model training on large-scale clusters.

Lien et al. (2024) employs a split computing approach to reduce the overall DNN training time for next-generation smart networks to enable the support for AI applications. A multi-tier DRL scheme obtains the distribution of the DNN model between the user device and edge, considering the model accuracy, energy consumption, and training latency, respectively. A DNN partitioning and scheduling scheme in heterogeneous IoT environments is designed to reduce training cost (time) and enhance resource utilization of resource-constrained devices (Huang et al., 2024). The solution adopts a double network policy gradient-based model comprising a stage partitioning network (employs fully connected networks) and a stage scheduling network to adaptively split the DL model into sub-stages and efficiently schedule these stages (or replicas) on end devices. Identically, Al Maruf et al. (2024) discussed a mechanism for DNN model parallelism to address the issues of optimal DNN model partition determination and their distribution among embedded devices to enhance DNN training performance. The solution jointly utilizes the characteristics of data and model parallelism, as well as pipeline-based execution, to attain higher training efficiency. The framework employs two modules: (i) identifies optimal DNN split by considering the model layers, processors’ computing capacities, computational needs of various DNN layers, and communication latency (ii) minimizes the model training time using pipeline-based model parallelism. The inclusion of pipeline-based execution caters to resource under-utilization of the task dependency scenarios for model parallelism, where processors rely on each other for inputs.

## 7.2.3. Split federated learning

In general, FL converges more quickly than SL, while SL tends to require fewer client-side resources than FL. For instance, Guo et al. (2021) designed LightFed, a novel federated edge learning system to cope with communication and data privacy issues. The framework consists of a model-splitting mechanism that enables the edge nodes to transmit only partial models. Further, a Selective Parameter Transmission Protocol (SPT) is devised to automatically detect and filter out parameter vectors with large gradients, reducing communication costs. Bharti and Mcgibney (2021) utilized the characteristics of SL to design a framework to train the DNN at resource-deprived EDs for collaborative predictive maintenance in smart manufacturing. Liu et al. (2022) discussed a distributed learning algorithm named hybrid split and federated learning (HSFL), comprising the parallel learning and structure splitting features of FL and SL to train the DNN in a distributed fashion in wireless UAV networks. It only shares model parameters that enable data privacy. The authors also designed a multi-arm-banditbased user device selection scheme by considering device computing capability, available channel condition, and the significance of local model updates to enhance the performance of FL. Deng et al. (2022) designed a compute-communication efficient and distributed framework to execute resource-hungry FL on resource-deprived IIoT devices. It splits the DNN model, which allows the training of lower layers at IoT devices, and offloads the higher layers to the ES for training. Moreover, data heterogeneity plays a vital role in the FL’s efficiency, and it degrades in the presence of non-IID data distribution. To this end, the authors devised a participating device selection scheme, which selects an IoT device (based on data distribution characteristics) among the available devices to improve the FL enactment.

Cheng et al. (2023) designed a distributed clustering-based HFSL framework for edge networks. The learning clients can create a cluster with their neighbors (or helping) clients through D2D communications to complete collaborative-FL. The framework splits the DNN model and allocates the partitioned segment to cluster members. Next, in each cluster, a transmission client is determined, which transmits the locally trained model of that cluster to the server for global model aggregation. The solution employs a game-theoretic approach, a heuristic solution, and a greedy-based convex optimization for cluster formation, DNN splitting and allocating splinted segments to cluster members, and transmission client selection, respectively. Feng et al. (2023) discussed an efficient distributed learning framework, IoTSL, using cloud–edge collaboration in IoT systems to address SL’s higher communication, computation, and energy consumption. The work utilizes the fundamental concepts of SL to split the DNN model layers among the participating devices. Xu et al. (2023a) designed an SFL framework that employs the features of parallel training of FL and model splitting of SL to train an efficient global DNN model. The author formulates the problem as a joint optimization of splitting point selection and bandwidth allotment for minimizing the training latency and discusses an alternating optimization-based solution proposed for the same. Khan et al. (2023) discussed the hierarchical split FL, a novel framework that enables distributed DNN model training at resource-constrained end devices. It operates on the grouping principle, where, each group comprises a set of end devices and an ES. The DNN model is split between the ES and end devices to minimize the computation overhead on end devices. In each group, the ES aggregates the local partial models of end devices and transmits the updated model parameters back to end devices to update the model accordingly. The procedure follows iteratively for some epochs. Further, the solution produces the global model by aggregating the local model of ESs. Chen et al. (2023c) discussed a novel DNN training approach by utilizing the concept of SL to train a DNN model in the network of distributed heterogeneous devices with a fault-tolerance mechanism. The solution dynamically determines the DNN splitting points by considering the devices’ real-time computing capacities.

Guo et al. (2023) described a SFL scheme that enables the DNN co-training between resource poor EDs and server to minimize the total system latency. The solution jointly optimizes the partition layer selection and bandwidth allotment in SFL, minimizing total latency. It decomposes this into two sub-problems, where the solution of the first subproblem identifies the DNN split point, and the allocation of efficient communication resources is obtained by solving the second sub-problem. It also comprises a mixed global and local model parameter replication process that periodically backs up the model parameters to cope with scenarios such as worker failure or network disconnection to handle faults during DNN training. EcoFed (Wu et al., 2024a) is the first framework designed to effectively decrease communication overheads of DNN Partitioning-based FL (DPFL) using novel approaches for optimizing forward and backward passes in DPFL. EcoFed uses an activation switch to decide whether outputs from the device-side model need to be sent to the server or if the server can use the cached activation to train the server-side model from the replay buffer. Further, compression and quantization are used for the activation outputs when transmitting from the end device to ES. The solution updates the replay buffer with compressed and quantized data, which is reconstructed through the decompressor module. Thus, EcoFed minimizes the communication cost of DPFL by eradicating the gradient communication, adjusting activations’ transmission frequency, and squeezing the activation data. Zhang et al. (2024b) designed a communicationefficient federated aggregation algorithm named FedSL, to diminish the communication overhead of FL-based DNN training while guaranteeing the performance of global model. The solution transmits fewer neural network parameters to reduce network data traffic. It determines the similarities of different layers of CNN models in FL to avoid redundant information in the feature extractor model, resulting in the transmission of lesser model parameters.

SL-based FL solution is designed for the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) network by Ni et al. (2024) considering collaborative healthcare analytics framework to realize the training of the DL model in the eco-system of resource-deprived devices. The solution supports semidistributed learning and preserves user data partially. It partitions the DNN model into two sub-networks, where high-level layers are executed on the server side, and lower layers are executed on IoMT devices to enable model training in deice-server synergy without revealing sensitive raw data. Wu et al. (2024b) discussed an SFL scheme for vehicular edge networks where the vehicular device’s total cost was the weighted sum of the training time and energy consumption. The authors utilize a dynamic-step Q-learning-based RL solution to obtain the near-optimal allocation of the computation and communication resources and split the DNN between vehicular devices and ES. Arouj and Abdelmoniem (2024) designed EAFL+, a novel solution, to support computational intensive and delay-sensitive tasks in cloud and MEC-enabled environments. The approach integrates the SL paradigm with FL training to attain energy-aware training. SL-based computation offloading reduces low-energy devices’ computational burden and enables them to participate in FL procedures in a heterogeneous computing environment. Hence, it enhances total training performance and lowers the dropout of clients. Luo and Zhang (2024) discussed an efficient distributed DNN training framework, which integrates the features of FL, SL, and mutual KD, respectively.

Discussion: The distributed edge training solution enable the collaboratively DNN training across multiple EDs, rather than depending on a centralized server. It leverages the computational power and data of multiple distributed EDs to accomplish DNN training task. Thus distributed edge training offers significant benefits in terms of privacy, scalability, fault tolerance, and resource utilization efficiency in compared to on-device edge learning. In the context of distributed DNN training enabler, FL-based solution obtains the global and generalized DNN model by aggregating the knowledge from local clients and SL-based solutions accomplish optimizes distributed DNN training.

FSL-based solutions leverage the features of SL and FL to train a generalized and optimized DNN model. The distributed training and privacy preserving features makes the FL as a potential model training solution across a wide range of applications. However, training a large DL model on resource-constrained node is still challenging. To this end, SL and FSL schemes realizes the distributed and privacy-preserving DL learning mechanism efficiently.

## 7.3. Data availability based DNN training in Edge-AI

The impact of data availability on DNN training tasks is overlooked in most of the Edge AI-based review articles. Thus, this section concerns the state-of-the-art methods of data availability based DNN training procedures, including Transfer Learning (TL) and Continual Learning (CL). Fig. 9 depicts the taxonomy of DNN training task under the limited data availability. TL enables the multi-task multi-model phenomena, which trains a new DNN for each new task with limited data. In contrast, CL enables multi-task single-model phenomena, which accommodates the knowledge of current task with limited data with the previously learned tasks in a single DNN. TL and CL solutions utilize the schemes depicted in Fig. 8 to accomplish DNN training task under limited data availability.

## 7.3.1. Transfer learning in Edge-AI

The DL techniques are data-dependent and thus mandate huge amounts of training data to train it from scratch. However, availability of limited labeled data is a major challenge in various applications such as smart health, manufacturing, etc. This issue of DL training with limited data can be preached by TL (Pan and Yang, 2009). TL has displayed remarkable capabilities in acquiring satisfactory DL performance through finetuning a DL model pre-trained for other tasks. Hou et al. (2020) deployed inspection robots and TL-based cascade mask region CNN on robots for identifying stay cable surface defects. Compared to traditional image processing and CNN, it offers a more accurate method for inspecting, precisely locating, and measuring surface defects. Wang et al. (2021) conducted a study detecting bearing faults in different working conditions having difficulty in obtaining large-scale labeled datasets. They employed the TL approach to transfer knowledge of fault diagnosis models trained under a single working condition to different working conditions with limited data samples. They combined a deformable CNN and deep LSTM for rolling bearing fault diagnosis. Xia et al. (2021) offered a DL solution for machine fault diagnosis, integrating Digital Twin (DT) and TL under limited labeled or inaccessible data for fault conditions of machines with differing working or shifted attributes. They validated it through a triplex pump fault diagnosis and developed a sparse denoising AutoEncoder (AE) that finetunes this model from the source domain to target working conditions. A TL-based edge-CNN framework was proposed by Yang et al. (2021a) for industrial edge networks to accomplish a balance between energy consumption, latency, and model performance. Yang et al. (2021b) integrated TL and an ensemble learning classifier to improve wind turbine blade damage recognition performance. This work employed TL to extract abstract features and fasten convergence efficiency. The random forest-based ensemble learning classifier improves the accuracy of detecting blade defects. Zim et al. (2023) developed a DNN model embedding TL for predicting the material removal rate of the electrical discharge machining process, which overcomes the requirement of large volumes of training data. Lu et al. (2022) developed an ensemble TL approach incorporating TrAdaBoost-R2, Bayesian, and Markov chain Monte Carlo calibration for cutting energy consumption prediction. Moreover, Lockner and Hopmann (2021) used induced network-based TL to reduce the huge training data requirement for an AI model trained for the injection molding process. They observed significant disparities in the transferability of the source model for varying geometries; likewise, similar geometries to the target process achieve the best performance parameters. TL can be validated using different DL models to detect defects in steel surfaces, wherein the MobileNet model for TL achieved the best detection rate of 80.41% and 96.94% for the SEVERSTAL and NEU datasets, respectively (Abu et al., 2021).

## 7.3.2. Continual learning in Edge-AI

In many applications, the training data used for the model and the new data often do not align. Therefore, a new model must be trained from scratch using new data or for a new task. Continual Learning (CL) is a recent learning paradigm that trains models over time to gain insight for new tasks and retain proficiency for previous tasks (Wang et al., 2024c). Thus, CL methods find a balance in the stability and plasticity of DL weights or parameters while training for new tasks under the unavailability of the previous task data. Recently, a few examples of CL have been encountered in EC and manufacturing separately, such as Tham and Sharma (2021) presented particle filter algorithm-based CL for the prescriptive maintenance method in a distributed factory environment. They used the partially observable MDP framework, and the overall system allows for the unique adaptation of each machine. Tercan et al. (2022) addressed the challenge of quality prediction of continuously changing production processes (manufacturing of new products) using the CL method named memory-aware synapses. The proposed mechanism successfully prevents the DL model from forgetting previous knowledge and improves the training performance for the new regression tasks in injection molding.

Nain et al. (2023) proposed a CL-enabled DNN model to be proficient in handling the changing predictive quality of multiple processes. To reduce the impact of task orders in sequentially arriving CL tasks, they introduced a lightweight similarity analysis module at the resource-limited edge. The proposed module can reduce task order’s impact by 70% on the DNN performance after the fifth task in the use-case of plastic bricks. In their other work, the authors assumed different task importance under a mass production and customization scenario and developed a hybrid CL scheme named PackMASNet for the edge devices (Nain et al., 2024b). Maschler et al. (2022) discussed different regularization-based CL approaches on a real battery wear dataset, among which online elastic weight consolidation delivered the best performance. They further implemented CL approaches on NASA turbofan engine dataset and showed promising results for DL training (Maschler et al., 2020). Li et al. (2024b) developed a novel pseudo-replay CL method under the class incremental framework for anomaly detection.
